nock runs on your computer, and what it records about your project stays in your repository. We, Parallel Labs in Barcelona, receive three things, each only if you choose to send it: a weekly usage report of counts and categories under a random project id, if you said yes when nock asked; a reflection round's summary, when you send one after seeing it; and a report you write with `nock report`, with nock's version and your platform.

None of them holds code, file paths, file or tool names, prompts, or what your decisions say. `nock usage` shows what the next report would carry; `share = false` in `.nock/config.toml` stops the reports.

The website sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other sites; GitHub, which hosts it, sees your IP address. It keeps one thing in your browser, and only if you turn this summary off: that choice.

Our server is in the EU (Scaleway, France). It stores reports and summaries without your IP address, for up to 12 months, and keeps its access logs, with IP addresses, for up to 30 days. nock's agents run under your own coding agent's account; what they read goes to its provider, not to us.

To get a copy of your data, or have it corrected or deleted, write to [support@logika.host](mailto:support@logika.host) with your project's id from `.nock/project.json`; we answer within a month. You can complain to the Spanish data protection authority (AEPD) or your own.
