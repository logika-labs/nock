The toolbox your agent was missing.

Software engineering is not so much about writing code anymore – it's about managing complexity with our coding agents. nock is a framework that records their work, lets you review it and helps you reflect on how to make it better.

Reflection Agents that write the tools they need.

nock instruments your agents: every call and session is recorded on your machine, never committed. Every week or so a reflection round reads that record and the work since. It writes the tools that would have saved turns, repairs the ones that misbehave and retires the ones nobody uses, and the next round holds each change to what it expected.

Reflection rounds Review You judge what they made, when it suits you.

Agents never wait on you. They open a topic for each piece of work and put in it the decisions they made, with the alternatives they weighed, and what they made to show it: screenshots, texts, pages. In the studio's Work tab you accept, reject, or chat about each; what you reject comes back as a new version.

Review on your time Evaluation Know whether a change made them better.

A new tool, brief or model: does it make your agents better? An evaluation answers with real runs. It is a scenario in git beside the code, its variants are parameters, and every output is cached by its content, so the next question costs only what changed. The studio's Experiment tab draws it as a tree of runs and counts each measure per variant.

Evaluations nock in Logika The reflection round that catches bugs before they happen.

Logika is observability and analytics for coding agents. It runs its reflection rounds on nock: in the logika round, your agent reads Logika's evidence map, what each event logs and what could go wrong with it, and writes the rules with you from real captures. Logika then checks every span against them, so a bug is caught as an issue with its evidence before anyone notices it.

Built with the people who use it.

Engineers using nock add what they learn to it: new insights, features and reflection rounds. A way to share yours and take part is coming soon. Meanwhile, tell us from inside nock: .nock/nock report "…" shows you what it would send, and sends it on your word.

Getting started

From uvx nock init to your first verdict on what an agent made: seven steps, about ten minutes, in a repository of your own.

  • Step 1

    Install it in your repository

    Inside the repository, run uvx nock init (or pipx run nock init). It vendors nock into .nock/core/, writes .nock/config.toml, a primer CLAUDE.md imports, Claude Code's hooks and permissions, and the skills.

  • Step 2

    Commit it

    nock is your project's own code now, so it goes in git. Telemetry stays out: .nock/local/ is ignored, and never leaves your machine.

  • Step 3

    Make your tests the gate

    Add your test suite and linters as checks. Tools and reflection rounds must pass them, and .nock/nock check prints nothing when they do.

  • Step 4

    Work as you always do

    Start Claude Code and ask for something. Every session reads nock's primer through CLAUDE.md, starts with the project's tools listed once it has some, and is recorded: what you asked, each tool call, what failed, what it cost.

  • Step 5

    Your agent puts its work before you

    It opens a topic for the work, carries on, and submits what a later reader would ask "why" about: the choice it made, with what it weighed instead. It never waits on you.

  • Step 6

    Open the studio

    Run .nock/nock studio. Its Work tab lists the topics, newest activity first: what each holds, its verdicts, where the work stands.

  • Step 7

    Give your first verdict

    Open the decision, read the answer and what was considered instead, and accept it — or reject it, and the agent reworks it as its thread says. The first time, nock asks once whether to share a weekly usage report of counts; nothing is sent unless you say yes.

    • What happens next

    After 15 sessions or 7 days, a session start says a reflection round is due; run /nock-reflect in a session, and read its report. Your agents' tools grow from there.
